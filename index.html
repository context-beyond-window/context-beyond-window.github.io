Context Beyond the Window

Compression, Accumulation, and Internalization of Knowledge in Language Models

COLM 2026 Workshop · October 9, 2026 · Hilton Union Square, San Francisco

About

Modern language models operate within finite context windows, yet many real-world tasks require models to absorb, retain, and act on information that far exceeds any single prompt. This workshop addresses the full spectrum of context management: fitting more into the window, maintaining state across interactions, and transferring knowledge into parameters.

We frame this around the trade-off between context-time memory (information supplied at inference) and weight-time memory (information absorbed into parameters). Our goal is to build a shared vocabulary across subcommunities that rarely meet in one venue: long-context modeling, retrieval-augmented systems, continual learning, knowledge distillation, and LLM agents.

Topics of interest

  • Context compression and summarization
  • Long-context and infinite-context architectures
  • Memory-augmented and recurrent models
  • KV cache optimization
  • Retrieval-augmented generation
  • Multi-turn and multi-session context management
  • Agentic memory and orchestration
  • Knowledge distillation and internalization
  • Continual and lifelong learning
  • State space models
  • Test-time training and memorization
  • Benchmarks and evaluation

Speaker bios
Yoshua Bengio is a Turing Award laureate, Professor at Université de Montréal, Founder of Mila, and Scientific Director of LawZero. His recent work focuses on AI safety, including managing the risks of advanced AI systems, and on how language models can safely acquire, store, and act on knowledge.
Avi Singh is a Research Scientist at Google DeepMind (PhD, UC Berkeley). He co-authored the Many-Shot In-Context Learning paper demonstrating how expanded context windows enable significant performance gains without fine-tuning, bridging RL and long-context LLM capabilities.
Aakanksha Chowdhery is Co-Founder of Reflection AI and Adjunct Professor at Stanford. She was technical lead on PaLM (540B) and lead researcher for Gemini pre-training at Google, bringing deep expertise in large-scale pre-training and knowledge internalization.
Omar Khattab is an Assistant Professor at MIT EECS & CSAIL and creator of ColBERT and DSPy. His research directly addresses retrieval-augmented pipelines—how external knowledge is retrieved, compressed, and fed into language model context.
Yejin Choi is the Dieter Schwarz Foundation Professor at Stanford CS, Senior Director at NVIDIA Research, and a TIME100 AI honoree. Her research spans commonsense reasoning and knowledge representation, with a focus on scaling intelligence via smarter algorithms rather than brute-force context expansion.
Albert Gu is an Assistant Professor at CMU and Chief Scientist at Cartesia AI. He created the S4 and Mamba architectures for linear-time sequence modeling, foundational to how context can be compressed and managed beyond the transformer paradigm.

Panel

The Memory Bottleneck: When Should Knowledge Live in Context vs. Weights?

Moderator: Dwarkesh Patel — host of the Dwarkesh Podcast (TIME100 AI 2024), author of The Scaling Era (Stripe Press, 2025).

In his widely discussed essay “Thoughts on AI Progress” (December 2025), Patel argued that continual learning—the ability to move knowledge from context into weights—is the critical unsolved bottleneck for AI progress, sparking a multi-month debate among ML researchers. He brings deep technical preparation and a talent for surfacing disagreements between experts, having conducted in-depth interviews with researchers at the frontier of scaling (Sutskever, Amodei), interpretability (Douglas, Bricken), and architecture design (Karpathy).

The panel brings together all six invited speakers to debate: when should knowledge be compressed in context, retrieved from external stores, or internalized into weights? What are the safety implications of each? And is a unified framework possible?

Call for Papers

Submission format

  • Short papers: up to 4 pages (excluding references and appendix)
  • Long papers: up to 8 pages (excluding references and appendix)
  • All submissions are non-archival
  • Submission portal: OpenReview (link TBA)

What we accept

  • New research results
  • Position papers
  • System descriptions
  • Benchmark papers
  • Negative or synthetic findings that clarify trade-offs

Review process

Each submission will receive at least two reviews. The process is double-blind. Organizers will not review papers for which they have a conflict of interest. Accepted work will be presented as contributed talks or posters, with oral slots selected to ensure strong representation of junior researchers.
